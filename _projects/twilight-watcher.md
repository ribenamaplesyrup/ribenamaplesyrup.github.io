During the 2020 lockdowns, I discovered a website streaming footage from thousands of unsecured security cameras around the world. I was drawn in by the hypnotic quality of these static scenes. Deserted car parks, storefronts with an occasional shop-keeper wearing a mask, empty landscapes. The quietly dramatic changes in light at sunrise and sunset were often the most interesting event broadcasted from at each location.
![Report](/images/projects/twilight-watcher/mirror.png){: .img-three-quarters}
Inspired by the arsthetics of this 'slow cinema', I began analysing the transformations in colour from CCTV footage at twilight. Focusing on a weather camera at the Calar Alto Observatory in Spain, I programmed my Raspberry Pi computer to record the landscape each evening. I then applied colour-sorting algorithms to the footage, generating unique patterns that reveal the subtle variations between nights. The results demonstrated how small differences in weather, atmospheric conditions, and digital artifacts could render entirely distinct chromatic signatures.
