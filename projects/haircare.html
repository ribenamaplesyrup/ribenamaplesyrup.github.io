Productive City Hair Care


‘Productive City Hair Care’ is a proposal detailing how excess human hair within urban areas can be repurposed to improve the viability of food systems and other essential industries.

By 2030, the 5.2 billion humans inhabiting the earth's cities will produce an estimated 400,000 tonnes of hair each year, which equates to 34% of current global clean wool produced by a population of 1 billion sheep yearly. Human’s continued treatment of their hair as a waste product calls into question the efficiency of their expanding cities.

Hair is a rich source of keratin and can be used for many other things. Food production, oil spill remediation, medicines, and construction are industries that could be made more vibrant and viable by ingesting hair. The evolution of hair from a material for bodily protection to individual expression makes it all the harder for humans to recognise their hair’s potential.

Productive City Hair Care repurposes plugged drains, barber’s dustbins and saturated brushes of cities as an efficient patchwork of allotment spaces that comprise an urban farm in anagen. It saves the hair from becoming waste, takes advantage of existing modes of clean-up, and doesn’t require expensive equipment or fancy ingredients.


Productive City Hair Care will be exhibited at the Talin Architecture Bienalle 2022 as part of the Future Food Deal Competition. Within this project my contributions involved research, writing and systems design.
